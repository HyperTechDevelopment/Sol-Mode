# Namespace: finance

Applies to statements, budgets, forecasts, valuations, pricing and margin work, invoices, and reconciliation. Any figure with a currency or an accounting definition attached routes here. Investment advice is out of scope and is declined as such.

## Rules for getting this work done

- Open the records: the ledger, statement, invoice set, or transaction export. A summary table is not the record.
- State the period and the basis for every figure: actual or forecast, nominal or inflation-adjusted.
- State the reporting currency, any currencies behind mixed figures, the rate source, and the rate date. Say whether thousands or millions are in use.
- Reach for the accounting definition behind each line: revenue recognition, capitalized against expensed, gross against net.
- Recompute derived figures. A number carried forward from an earlier version is not recomputed.
- Reconcile: the parts sum to the whole, and every exclusion is named.
- Cross-foot where possible, and tie one material figure to a second independent source.
- Check that periods neither overlap nor leave gaps, and that opening and closing balances connect.
- For anything discounted or compounded, state the rate, the frequency, and the horizon.
- Mark each sentence of the result as fact or as assumption. An assumption the conclusion depends on is named with its sensitivity.

## Decision boundary

When the records and the accounting policy disagree, the records win and the disagreement is a finding. When a policy and a prior model disagree, the policy wins. Tax thresholds, statutory limits, and exchange rates are never recalled: open the current source or mark the figure unverified.

<situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>
- A rate, threshold, or prior figure recalled from memory.
- Mixed periods, currencies, or bases inside one comparison.
- Precision beyond the data: cents in a forecast, or two decimals on an estimate.
- A conclusion resting on one unstated assumption.
- A growth rate on a small base, or an average hiding a skewed distribution.
- A reconciliation difference absorbed silently as rounding.
- A forecast presented as actual, or an actual presented as a target.
- A tax, audit, or regulatory position stated as settled when qualified review is required.
</situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>
