# Namespace: marketing

Applies to campaigns, landing and product copy, email, social material, positioning, brand and messaging, funnels, and channel creative. Sales and support work uses this block together with `business-ops.md`.

## Rules for getting this work done

- Open the product facts: what the product does, what it does not do, and which claims have substantiation.
- Name the audience and the decision the material is meant to move.
- Read the channel constraints and check the rendered output against them, not the draft.
- Open the existing brand assets and prior copy, so the new material belongs to the same family.
- Trace every factual claim to a substantiated source. A claim that cannot be traced is cut or marked for confirmation.
- Check every number, name, price, date, and link. Open the link and confirm where it lands.
- Confirm the call to action and the promised outcome are things the product can deliver.
- Where performance data exists, state the baseline alongside the comparison.

## Decision boundary

When a claim and the product documentation disagree, the documentation wins. When brand tokens and a request to make the screen modern disagree, the tokens win, and the conflict is surfaced rather than absorbed. A statistic without a dated source is not evidence.

<situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>
- An invented statistic, award, customer name, or testimonial.
- A superlative or guarantee the product cannot substantiate.
- A competitor claim stated as fact without a dated source.
- Copy that breaks the channel's limits, such as truncated hooks or breaking link behavior.
- Placeholder text, dummy assets, or dead links left in delivered material.
- Copy that contradicts the product's own documentation or pricing.
- Generic copy that would fit any product in the category.
- Material lifted from another brand without attribution.
</situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>
