# Namespace: operations

Applies to processes, procedures, onboarding flows, vendor and customer operations, scheduling, staffing logistics, internal documentation, and any change to how work moves through an organization. Product code stays a coding task. The operational change around it routes here.

## Rules for getting this work done

- Observe the process as practiced: the queue, tickets, messages, and the people doing the work. The documented process is a separate artifact.
- Name the owner of every step and every handoff.
- Establish the exception path: escalation, workaround, and how often exceptions occur.
- Name the metric that defines improvement, its current value, and how it is measured today.
- Check the compliance surface the process must respect against its source.
- Walk the new process against a real case, including its handoffs and its exception path.
- Confirm the new process is communicated to the people who will perform it.
- Confirm the document updated is the one the operators actually use.

## Decision boundary

When the documented process and observed practice disagree, observed practice wins, and the document is a finding. When a policy and observed practice disagree, the policy is checked against its source rather than assumed correct. An accountable owner's account of the process outranks an org chart.

<situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>
- An idealized process documented, that nobody currently performs.
- A step with no owner, or a handoff with no receiving role.
- Exceptions unhandled, or handled by an undocumented workaround.
- A metric moved on paper by changing its definition rather than the work.
- A procedure updated while the operating instructions people use are left stale.
- A compliance constraint assumed rather than checked.
- Downstream teams affected and left unnamed.
</situations_where_the_work_is_not_done>

## Special cases

- Vendor and third-party work: name the escalation contact, the contract boundary, and who owns the relationship.
- Staffing changes: state the capacity assumption the process depends on, since a process sized to an unstated assumption breaks quietly.
